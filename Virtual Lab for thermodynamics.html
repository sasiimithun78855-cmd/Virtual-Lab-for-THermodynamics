Thermodynamics – Laws, Free Energy and Hess’s Law

Module III – Interactive Problem-Solving Lab

Aim

To learn the fundamentals of chemical thermodynamics – system classification, the Zeroth, First, Second and Third laws, entropy and free energy – to solve numerical problems with an interactive calculator, to apply Hess’s law of constant heat summation, and to assess understanding through a 10-question test.

Principle

Thermodynamics deals with energy changes in bulk (macroscopic) matter and depends only on the initial and final states of a system, not on its atomic structure or on the path followed.

ΔU = q + w  |  ΔH = ΔU + PΔV  |  ΔS = qrev/T  |  ΔG = ΔH − TΔS

Spontaneity (Gibbs energy)

ΔG < 0 spontaneous; ΔG > 0 non-spontaneous; ΔG = 0 equilibrium. Also ΔG = −nFE links free energy to cell EMF.

Hess’s law

The total enthalpy change of a reaction is the same whether it occurs in one step or several, because enthalpy is a state function.

Step 2 – Laws of Thermodynamics (simulate each, then answer the checkpoint)

Zeroth law – thermal equilibrium

If two bodies are each in thermal equilibrium with a third body, they are in equilibrium with each other. This is the basis of the thermometer.

Thermometer C reading: –

First law – conservation of energy

Energy can neither be created nor destroyed. ΔU = q − W (W done by system) or ΔU = q + w (w done on system); w = −PΔV. For a cyclic process ΔU = 0, and ΔU = q at constant volume.

Limits: the first law gives no direction of change, does not tell whether a process is spontaneous and cannot explain why heat is not completely converted to work → need for the second law.

Second law – entropy always increases

For a spontaneous (irreversible) process ΔSuniverse = ΔSsys + ΔSsurr > 0. Clausius: heat cannot flow from cold to hot without other change. Kelvin: heat cannot be completely converted into work without other changes. Entropy: gas > liquid > solid.

Heat flow ΔSuniverse

Third law – absolute zero entropy

The entropy of a perfect crystal is zero at absolute zero (0 K). Elements in standard states at 298 K do not have zero entropy. Unit: J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. S = k ln W.

Step 4 – Hess’s Law of Constant Heat Summation

Statement

The total enthalpy change for a reaction is independent of the route (one step or many) – it depends only on initial and final states, because H is a state function.

  1. Reverse a reaction → change sign of ΔH.
  2. Multiply by n → multiply ΔH by n.
  3. Add the equations; species on both sides cancel; ΔH values add.

Worked idea (slide example)

C + ½O₂ → CO (−110.5) and CO + ½O₂ → CO₂ (−283.0) give C + O₂ → CO₂, ΔH = −393.5 kJ/mol (exothermic).

ΔHrxn = Σ ΔHf(products) − Σ ΔHf(reactants)

ΔH negative = exothermic; positive = endothermic.
